# Twenty Worlds: source observations translated into build instructions

These notes were made from the original `examples/<world>/index.html`, `style.css`, `main.js`, selected page modules, and `docs/briefs/*.md` in Dioramas. A brief describes intent; the example code shows the shipped mechanism. The entries below favor behavior visible in source. They are **transferable direction**, not permission to copy a fictional brand, model, photo, sound, text, or source file. The installed skill must work without the Dioramas folder.

For each new website, choose **one** primary world mechanism and one compatible secondary detail. Never combine all twenty into a single page. Write a new subject-specific scene plan and create or license its assets.

## 01. Reliquary — light as navigation and discovery

The page opens in an almost unlit stone hall. The WebGL canvas stays behind the story as the visitor moves past relic stations; the DOM carries membership, hours, and links. The camera moves down the hall rather than cutting between independent product renders. A hand-held lantern follows the viewer with a little lag. When nobody moves the pointer, it searches around the current relic so the composition does not die in darkness. The light pool reveals both the 3D exhibit and parts of the hero typography. Photographing a relic changes the exhibit state: its lamp remains on and a Polaroid enters a journal. The important chain is **aim → find → document → permanent world change**. The code projects relic cards from 3D world positions and pauses expensive rendering when opaque content panels cover the canvas. The typography is large Cormorant serif with restrained Instrument Sans and JetBrains Mono labels; brass against deep brown-black. For a new site, build a light that changes what can be understood, and make the action possible by an explicit button as well as pointer aim. A flashlight circle without a discoverable object or state change is decoration.

## 02. Abyssal — scroll as a physically changing environment

The camera and submersible travel through named depth stations. The gauge reports depth while environmental variables change: shallow caustics and bubbles yield to denser fog, a narrowing spectrum, marine snow, then floodlights and a beacon. The source maps scroll to a continuous station value, then blends camera position/look/fov with a wider arc mid-transition so the passage feels like a dive. Creature poses are framed for their station cameras and kept clear of HUD and copy. In dark stations the pointer steers floodlights within limits; an idle search behavior keeps subjects lit when the pointer is absent. Sonar is attached to `click`, not `pointerdown`, so touch scrolling cannot trigger it. HUD contacts, dive log, pressure, and temperature update with depth. Space Grotesk plus IBM Plex Mono makes the instrument readouts feel native to the vessel. For a new site, define a table of environmental states, not merely a background gradient: camera, fog, key light, subject behavior, particles, audio, and readout should respond to the same depth value. Test reverse scroll and direct jumps to every station.

## 03. Ember — the subject changes material over time

The forge is a complete set: coals, tools, wall, anvil, hot and cool light sources. Scroll moves through forge stations and changes the sword pose and heat, with a cold-front quench tied to scroll so a refresh midway still yields the right state. Material work matters: heat propagates along the blade; emissive steel, flakes, sparks, steam, and nearby light all respond to the same thermal state. Pointer or keyboard input strikes the sword. The hammer has anticipation, impact, and recovery; impact drives spark velocity, flash, camera shake, a fold counter, and optional audio. Holding the bellows raises heat. A finished blade appears in a calmer macro composition at the end. Oswald's compressed lettering, Crimson Pro, bone type, and molten orange suit the physical weight. For a new site, make an interaction alter the product material and environment, then show a consequence in a later section. One orange light or particle burst does not explain a transformation.

## 04. Terrarium — touch changes a transparent surface

The set is a bright linen room with a reflective window, walnut base, glass bell, miniature climate, and small life inside. The glass has a fog/clear state driven by a wipe mask; brushing the surface clears a trail, then condensation slowly returns. The code includes pointer and touch wipe paths and even draws a gentle initial streak so the affordance is legible before the visitor acts. Climate tabs trigger a sequence: lift glass, turn base, swap the world, reseat and refog the bell. Scroll later lifts the bell and separates substrate layers into an explanatory diagram, with labels anchored to 3D positions. Camera poses include a lens shift to keep the subject above copy on narrow screens. Fraunces plus Manrope and warm linen/sage/walnut keep it tactile and calm. For a new site, demonstrate the transparent material before building the wipe; glass needs reflections, something behind it, thickness, and a believable fogged state. A white opacity overlay is insufficient.

## 05. Koi — direct manipulation in a quiet world

The pond fills the viewport from a high angle. The visitor can disturb it: movement and taps produce ripples, food directs fish, and floating leaves/lotus respond. Koi swim below the water surface, whose distortion and reflection make them feel submerged rather than pasted on top. Scroll drifts the camera across the pond while the ryokan content passes through calmer paper-like sections. Sound and visual speed stay restrained. The source separates water, koi, maple, garden, and flora modules; this is a systems scene, not a single shader. Shippori Mincho and Zen Kaku Gothic New pair with washi panels and vermilion marks. For a new site, connect input to at least two layers of the ecosystem: touch should move water and alter an animal/object response. Bound the effect so the surface returns to stillness and CTA text remains readable.

## 06. Florae — object identity through particle morphing

The source samples colored positions from several 3D specimens and uses GPU computation to move particles between forms. The hero rose must read as a rose before a transition begins. Scroll changes specimens through staged turbulence and settling; a pointer breeze displaces nearby particles, while press introduces a stronger gust. Copy gives Latin name, origin, season, and archive context. The surrounding page is editorial cream with a Cormorant/EB Garamond serif voice and tight sans details; the point cloud is the focal object. There are separate specimen, archive, and subscription zones, not one endless particle loop. For a new site, prepare compatible source/target position and color data, assign stable particle IDs, and make the shape recognizable at rest. Transition parameters need lift, swirl, stagger, and a settle phase; direct linear lerp looks like a dissolve. On mobile, reduce simulation cost before reducing type clarity.

## 07. Velocity — reveal an alternate truth through a lens

The shoe is a lit product shot on a clinical stage. The lens is a distinct compositing system: the ordinary view remains outside, while X-ray, thermal, or CAD render modes appear inside a circular viewport. The CAD/thermal/plate geometry uses measured locations on the model rather than generic overlays. Mode buttons and keys 1–3 change the revealed information. Scroll rotates the shoe into plate, foam, upper, color, and launch stations. Callouts are projected from 3D anchors and adjusted to avoid colliding with the lens or text. When idle, the lens docks on an informative part of the shoe, away from copy. Archivo display, mono measurements, rulers, and a pale lab background make the inspection metaphor clear. For a new site, each mode must answer a different user question; a color filter alone is not an X-ray. Compile every render mode before exit from the loader.

## 08. Hangar — hold input unlocks a sequence, not a flash

The mech starts barely visible under emergency red light. Press and hold raises a gauge; crossing its threshold triggers bay lights in sequence, a visor glow, steam, HUD boot, scan plane, and reveal of materials. The source includes WebGL context-loss handling and compiles multiple visual states. Scroll then circles the machine through specification stations, with 3D-positioned hotspots, drone motion, and floor reflections. The industrial set includes gantries, wet concrete, haze, and moving beams. Chakra Petch/Rajdhani/Share Tech Mono typography supplies technical hierarchy; amber is reserved for the action. For a new site, define the hold state precisely: start, charge, threshold, completion, release before threshold, keyboard/touch equivalent, and reset. Expose the result of power-up in the scene, not just in a progress circle.

## 09. Sucre — physics becomes a product interaction

The page uses a pastry display set with marble counter, cake, soft daylight, and instanced objects. Physics places macarons and fruit on the counter, lets a fingertip nudge or pick and toss them, and makes a curated gift box an interaction with completion. The source distinguishes touch on a pastry from a normal page scroll. Menu hover brings the corresponding item forward on the counter. Scroll moves from hero to menu, box, atelier, and visit; camera stations are tailored for portrait. The cake remains a turning beauty object while smaller pastries do the physical work. Playfair/DM Sans, pale pink, pistachio, and gold make the collision behavior feel joyful rather than chaotic. For a new site, use simplified colliders and a bounded body count; physics needs a clear goal and a clean resting composition. A pile of random objects can obscure both product and CTA.

## 10. Monolith — one control changes the whole lighting system

The architecture model is treated as a study of daylight. The visitor drags a visible sun/time control through dawn, noon, golden hour, blue hour, and night. Light direction, intensity, color, sky, exposure, architectural shadows, window emission, and UI theme channels update from one time value. Scroll changes camera perspective for the hero, selected works, house plan/section/detail, principles, and contact. The camera can orbit an interest point while the building remains stable. At night, interior windows and pool become a second source of light. Inter Tight, IBM Plex Serif, strict grid, off-white, and one amber accent reflect the architectural discipline. For a new site, write a lighting-state table and derive both scene and readable HTML colors from it. If only the sky changes while shadows remain fixed, the concept fails.

## 11. Lowfreq — sound and object motion share one state

The turntable is in a dim listening room with a record wall, tube glow, haze, spotlight, and dust. Play spins the vinyl and starts audio after a user gesture. The platter, room light, speaker response, and spectrum are all tied to playback or analyser data. Dragging the record scratches it; releasing resumes normal motion. Tracklist controls cue the deck, while records in the catalogue behave as collectible sleeves rather than generic cards. The source builds the room, deck, audio, and UI as cooperating systems. Syne, Space Mono, deep aubergine, amber, and lavender make the analog music identity. For a new site, keep audio opt-in and make muted operation visibly complete. Bind the turntable angle, playback rate, spectrum, and light response to one playback state; independent loops will drift apart.

## 12. Gambit — scroll can replay discrete events

The chess board and Carrara/Nero pieces are a luxury stage with floor reflection and low lighting. The page maps scroll through the Immortal Game, a sequence of discrete moves rather than a continuous decorative orbit. The active notation, piece destinations, captures, camera focus, and narrative annotations track the same ply. Knights travel on arcs; captured pieces leave the board. The source uses normalized element progress for replay and later switches into free play with pick, drag, and square snap. It prevents touch scrolling while a piece is actively dragged. Bodoni, black/bone/brass, salon copy, and pricing keep the page anchored to a real fictional product. For a new site, precompute event positions and make the state deterministic from scroll progress so fast scrolling, reversing, and refresh never lose a piece. Clearly signal when replay control yields to free interaction.

## 13. Atlas — travel requires a path, not a camera teleport

Four islands float above a cloud sea. The visitor selects an island by clicking it or its map point; the camera flies on a curved route, banks into the turn, breathes its FOV, threads cloud wisps, and settles into a gentle orbit. Scroll also advances through destination stations. Islands bob, birds move, an airship crosses the sky, and destination tickets appear early enough to be readable as the camera arrives. Labels pin to islands; hit testing avoids interpreting a UI click as a world click. The source seeds cloud wisps along each path and calculates bank from lateral acceleration. Young Serif, Nunito Sans, sky blue/peach/cream, and ticket-like panels support the travel conceit. For a new site, specify departure, path curvature, arrival framing, and what the visitor can do during flight. Keep horizon and destination legible; provide a reduced-motion cut or short crossfade.

## 14. Lithos — a hold gesture earns a material reveal

The geode starts closed on a dark vitrine. Holding builds visible cracks, dust, tremor, and violet light before the halves separate to expose crystal. The opening should be staged; the inner cavity, specular glints, and ambient bounce must justify the reveal. A reseal action restores it. Later scroll stations present quartz, pyrite, and ammonite with different material treatments; the pointer moves a small inspection light to reveal facets. The code has release/cancel/blur paths for the hold gesture and a calm gallery layout for provenance and jewelry. Italiana/Cinzel/Jost, black velvet, platinum, and violet suit a museum collection. For a new site, make the revealed material worth seeing before tuning dust and shake. Keyboard and touch must be able to trigger the crack, and early release must leave a coherent sealed state.

## 15. Artemis — locomotion is authored against terrain

The lunar page places a walking astronaut on heightfield regolith with rocks, long shadows, a black sky, Earth, and an outpost. A long walk section controls the character's position while camera follow, pose, footprints, and dust respond. The source includes feed-forward to stop a damped follow camera lagging behind the astronaut and clamps camera height above terrain. It initializes footprints correctly even when loaded midway through the page. Later sections change vantage around the outpost and itinerary. The visual system is largely monochrome with mission orange and Space Grotesk/Barlow Condensed readouts. For a new site, the walk cycle, displacement, contact shadow, foot placement, and camera need one progress source. A floating walk animation over a plane will not feel grounded. Avoid physically forced motion for reduced-motion users; let them advance through stations discretely.

## 16. Keeper — atmosphere is part of a rescue task

The lighthouse stands in moving dark water with storm rain, clouds, lightning, and a sweeping lamp. The visitor can take the beam and hold it on a boat long enough to guide it home. This makes the beam a tool, not a visual accent. Scroll moves from storm through rooms/crossing to dawn; water and weather calm as the page progresses. The source handles beams, ocean, sky, audio, and effects in separate modules. The lamp color must read through rain without bleaching the rest of the frame. Libre Caslon and Karla, ink-black ocean, sodium gold, and pink dawn support the human story. For a new site, connect weather state, light sweep, boat visibility, rescue timer, and reward. Provide an explicit rescue control or alternate keyboard route; pointer search alone is not accessible.

## 17. Koen — one subject carries four seasonal material states

The same bonsai remains the protagonist across spring, summer, autumn, and winter. Scroll and a dial change leaf color/coverage, falling particles, light temperature, background, and shadow; winter reveals branches and snow rather than simply making the tree white. The source separates season, particles, dial, and tree behavior and updates CSS scene colors alongside WebGL. Text describes nursery, craft, and visit in a restrained paper layout. Cormorant/Noto Serif JP, washi, ink, and a red seal create a slow editorial rhythm. For a new site, use one normalized season value with four authored poses and explicit transition windows. A hue filter alone cannot show time passing; geometry, particles, and lighting must change too. Keep the dial synchronized with scroll so neither fights the other.

## 18. Nocturne — glass needs a world to refract

The bottle is a physical glass object on a dark reflective floor, with tall strip-light reflections, amber liquid, cap, and atmospheric glows. Drag tilts the bottle while the liquid surface stays roughly level in world space and lags with a damped slosh. Scroll introduces bergamot, iris, vanilla, and oud as floating notes; their matter dissolves into streams that enter the bottle. The source includes specialized bottle, stream, and floor modules and projects title position from camera geometry. Bodoni/Italiana, champagne gold, black, and careful strip highlights frame the product. For a new site, build a meaningful reflection/refraction environment before tuning glass transparency. The liquid needs a meniscus, apparent level, and response to angular velocity; a colored box inside clear glass is unconvincing. Provide a non-drag way to inspect notes.

## 19. Racer — one control links mechanical, visual, and audio response

The motorcycle is staged outside a workshop on wet asphalt with neon, shutter light, rain, reflection, exhaust, and headlight. Hold-to-rev drives a tachometer, bike vibration, alternator-fed headlight, exhaust flare, optional engine sound, and even subtle camera shake. Release produces deceleration and a short overrun. Scroll moves from hero to rev station to close studies of tank, engine, seat, and wheels, with depth of field on the relevant detail. The source builds an environment beyond the bike so reflective surfaces have something to show. Anton and Inter, black asphalt, hot magenta, brass, and a sparing script accent establish identity. For a new site, one RPM value must drive every visible mechanical response; separate random flickers feel disconnected. Audio must require a gesture and have an obvious mute.

## 20. Horologie — exploded views teach a real mechanism

The page begins with a watch as a premium object, then scroll moves inside an authored procedural movement. Layers separate along the axis, while gears, spring, escapement, jewels, and finishing surfaces retain recognizable relationships. The source has distinct modules for movement, geometry, shadow, finish, and studio setup; it uses depth of field to guide attention. Labels are tied to the relevant 3D part, and a crown hold winds a reserve gauge and changes movement behavior. Reverse scroll reassembles the mechanism. Cormorant Garamond/Montserrat, ivory/navy/rose gold, fine rules, and restrained technical labels feel like watchmaking. For a new site, define an exploded-state table that preserves part order and explanatory hierarchy. Pieces flying apart at random are less useful than a readable mechanical diagram. Compile assembled and exploded visual states before the loader exits.

## Shared mechanics found across the source

1. **Scene ownership.** A fixed canvas often remains visible through most sections while semantic HTML provides text, links, forms, and CTAs. The 3D subject is composed around the text column, not centered blindly.
2. **Camera stations.** Source pages author camera position, look target, FOV, subject pose, and effects for each section. Mid-transition arcs avoid clipping through the set; portrait screens often need a different pose or lens shift.
3. **One progress value per physical concept.** Depth, temperature, season, time of day, RPM, charge, replay ply, or liquid fill drives several systems at once. This creates causality.
4. **Real loading.** Assets load before exit; the scene and alternate materials are compiled before the first revealed frame. The loader has a visual metaphor native to the subject.
5. **Stateful input.** Direct interaction has affordance, active feedback, completion or limit, release/cancel behavior, and a way back. Touch scrolling and object drag are explicitly distinguished.
6. **Material and lighting first.** Reflective floors, glass, crystal, wet metal, and product PBR all depend on a world with motivated light sources; bloom is a finishing choice, not the source of quality.
7. **HTML art direction.** Each world uses its own type family, hierarchy, copy, palette, readout style, buttons, and navigation. Reusing one generic dark template would erase the design intent.
8. **Performance and access.** The source uses portrait framing, render pauses, bounded particle/physics systems, shader compilation, mobile considerations, and UI controls. A new build should also include reduced motion, keyboard routes, and a fallback poster when WebGL fails.
