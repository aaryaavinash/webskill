# Type has to belong to the subject

Choose faces by the world being built. The source collection demonstrates why one default font pair cannot serve all pages: Space Grotesk and IBM Plex Mono make Abyssal feel like an instrument; Fraunces and Manrope make Terrarium feel soft; Bodoni and Inter make Gambit ceremonial; Chakra Petch and Share Tech Mono make Hangar feel engineered; Shippori Mincho and Zen Kaku Gothic New make Koi slow and precise. These are observations, not a fixed font shopping list for new brands.

## Type roles

Define at least five roles: display hero, section heading, body, action label, and data/annotation. Choose one display family and one highly readable body family; a third mono face is useful only where measurements, timestamps, coordinates, or mechanical labels carry meaning. Set size, weight, line height, letter spacing, and maximum measure for each role. Give numbers tabular figures where changing values are displayed. Make the hero title wrap deliberately at desktop and portrait sizes; do not rely on chance line breaks or a fixed pixel width.

The hero title is a visual mass. Place it in relation to the subject silhouette: sometimes it overlaps a dark area, sometimes it frames the model from the opposite edge, and sometimes it is revealed by light. Avoid the generic trick of italicizing one arbitrary word. If a contrasting serif or italic appears, it must express the brand's voice or the meaning of the sentence.

## Type against moving imagery

Inspect text at every camera station and midpoint. A readable still can become illegible when a bright reflection or particle cloud passes behind it. Use a text-safe area, controlled gradient, local scrim, or scene repositioning. Do not solve all contrast problems by placing a large opaque rectangle over the world; that often kills the scene. HUD/readouts can be small but must remain legible on real devices. On mobile, simplify or relocate secondary labels before shrinking core body copy.

## Reveal choreography

Text motion should serve the story. A measurement may roll as depth/RPM changes; a title may appear as a lamp reaches it; a section heading can settle as the camera arrives. Use one or two motifs, not identical word-by-word reveals on every section. Never make essential information invisible until a user scrolls perfectly. Reduced motion should show the text immediately or with a restrained opacity transition. Split-text animation must preserve actual text for screen readers and should be rebuilt on resize if line wrapping changes.

## Content standard

Write specific copy about the subject. A credible cinematic site contains measurements, process details, provenance, timetable, pricing, or product features where appropriate. Do not fill sections with vague lines such as “Experience the extraordinary.” Every phrase should help a visitor understand the subject or choose an action. The CTA verb should be concrete.

## Review

Capture 360 px, 390 px, tablet, desktop, and wide desktop at all story stations. Check line breaks, orphan words, collision with 3D labels, focus outlines, text contrast, and whether a gauge changes width when digits roll. If the page reads like the same template with a new font, reconsider layout and verbal rhythm.
